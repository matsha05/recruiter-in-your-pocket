# Recruiter in Your Pocket — Homepage Story Arc

Last updated: 2026-03-21
Owner: Product + Design
Status: Canonical homepage narrative and visual arc

This document defines how the homepage should tell the story.

The homepage is not:
- a research archive
- a feature catalog
- a startup credibility wall
- a broad job-search platform pitch

The homepage is:
- the sharpest expression of the brand
- the fastest path to understanding the wedge
- the emotional bridge from anxiety to action

## 1. Core Narrative

The homepage should tell one story:

Your resume is being judged quickly.
We show you what a recruiter sees first.
We show the exact line creating drag.
We make the stronger version obvious.
You stay in control.

Everything else is support.

## 2. Emotional Arc

### Part 1: Tension

The user should feel:
- “Yes, that is true.”
- “This matters.”

### Part 2: Reveal

The user should feel:
- “This product can actually show me the hidden read.”

### Part 3: Transformation

The user should feel:
- “That rewrite is obviously better.”
- “They can show me exactly where the drag is.”

### Part 4: Trust

The user should feel:
- “This is honest.”
- “This is safe.”
- “This is not another vague AI paywall.”

### Part 5: Control

The user should feel:
- “I know what happens next.”
- “I still decide.”

## 3. Hero Doctrine

### 3.1 What The Hero Must Accomplish

In one viewport, the hero must communicate:

1. the problem
2. the wedge
3. the product is real
4. the next action

### 3.2 Hero Structure

Approved structure:
- one small framing tag
- one decisive headline
- one sentence of mechanism
- one direct primary CTA
- one restrained secondary trust or methodology link
- one narrow proof strip
- one artifact glimpse, supporting the headline

### 3.3 Hero Visual Recipe

#### Left Column
- framing tag
- dominant headline
- mechanism line
- primary CTA
- secondary CTA
- one support line or proof strip

#### Right Column
- partial or restrained report object
- one visible verdict
- one visible fix
- one visible evidence cue

The object should prove the product is real without stealing the first impression from the headline.

### 3.4 Hero Copy Territory

Lead from one territory only:

#### Territory A: Judgment Truth
- They are not reading your resume. They are deciding on it.
- Your resume gets judged fast. We show you what they see first.

#### Territory B: Hidden Read
- See what a recruiter sees first.
- The first-pass picture of you, made visible.

#### Territory C: Specific Fix
- Find the line that is costing you.
- See what weakens the first impression, then fix it.

### 3.5 Hero Proof Strip

Use one minimal strip that reinforces action and control.

Approved ingredients:
- first report free
- no login required
- save only if you want to

`7.4 seconds` should not lead the hero.
It belongs in the proof layer after interest is established.

## 4. Visual Arc Of The Page

### Opening

Tone:
- cooler
- sharper
- higher contrast

Purpose:
- establish seriousness
- make the headline lead
- make the product feel real

### Middle

Tone:
- explanatory
- exact
- product-first

Purpose:
- show the transformation
- explain the mechanism
- convert intrigue into belief

### Closing

Tone:
- calmer
- trust-heavy
- less dramatic

Purpose:
- remove fear
- explain free vs paid
- restate the action

## 5. Approved Section Order

### 1. Hero

Purpose:
- truth
- proof
- CTA

Visual priority:
- highest

### 2. Mechanism + Transformation

Purpose:
- show how the judgment works
- show the line-level transformation

Best content:
- before line
- why it drags
- stronger rewrite
- signal logic in plain English

This is the most important section after the hero.

### 3. Research As Proof

Purpose:
- support the product’s authority

Best content:
- one featured note
- two or three concise supporting links
- one clear takeaway per note

This section should feel supportive, not expansive.

### 4. Trust And Control

Purpose:
- remove fear around sensitive input

Best content:
- what is free
- when data is saved
- how deletion works
- methodology and privacy links

### 5. Pricing

Purpose:
- clarify boundaries

Best content:
- what the free report includes
- what paid unlocks
- clear plan differences

## 6. Research Placement Rule

Research should answer:
- why trust this read?

It should not try to answer:
- everything we know about hiring

If research visually outweighs the transformation or trust story, the homepage is telling the wrong story.

## 7. Trust Placement Rule

Trust must arrive before pricing feels transactional.

The user should understand:
- the first report is free
- no forced save
- no account required until needed
- methodology is available
- privacy is explicit

## 8. CTA System

### Primary CTA

Preferred:
- `Run free report`
- `Get your free report`

### Secondary CTA

Preferred:
- `See methodology`
- `See how it works`

### Avoid

- `Get started`
- `Learn more`
- multiple primary actions above the fold

## 9. Section Visual Weight Rules

Use this ranking when deciding what should dominate visually:

1. Hero headline
2. Hero artifact glimpse
3. Transformation section
4. Trust and control
5. Mechanism explanation
6. Research proof
7. Pricing details

If research, testimonials, or pricing visually outweigh the hero and transformation story, the page has drifted.

## 10. Homepage Copy Rules

### Use

- short claims
- concrete nouns
- exact outcomes
- contrast
- one thought at a time

### Avoid

- startup filler
- feature inventory language
- too many proof systems at once
- personality that weakens authority

- stacked reassurance
- system language
- generic AI phrasing
- "best-in-class" startup language
- too many metrics

## 11. Home Page Visual Rules

### Use

- one dominant artifact
- a restrained proof strip
- exact before/after moments
- section shifts that help pacing

### Avoid

- card grids of equal importance
- decorative quote walls
- hero stat trios that upstage the product
- visual gimmicks standing in for explanation

## 12. Emotional Pacing

The homepage should feel like:

### Above the fold
- sharp
- controlled
- slightly high-stakes

### Mid-page
- explanatory
- confidence-building

### Lower page
- safe
- transparent
- clear

The page should not stay at the same emotional temperature the entire time.

## 13. Story Failures To Avoid

Do not let the page become:

### A Research Brand Homepage

Symptom:
- too much editorial proof too early

### A Resume Tool Homepage

Symptom:
- too many features and categories

### A Boutique Product Homepage

Symptom:
- warmth and cleverness overpower utility

### A Pricing Trap Homepage

Symptom:
- free vs paid is still fuzzy after one scroll

## 14. The Final Homepage Test

If someone scrolls the page quickly, they should remember:

1. This shows what recruiters see first.
2. It points to the exact problem line.
3. The first report is free.
4. The product feels trustworthy.
5. The next step is obvious.
