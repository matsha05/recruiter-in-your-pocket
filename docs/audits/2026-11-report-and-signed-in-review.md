## Verdict

**Not yet, with the single biggest reason being the shape, not the writing.** For a mid-career resume with a few weak lines, the free report is worth the upload: on Maria's, James's, Wei's and the sample's resumes the Critical Miss is the line a good recruiter would have circled, the pen note says what to add, and the answer flow turns that into a line you can paste. That is better than anything a scanner gives, and the $29 re-check is the only honest proof loop in the category. But the report has one shape, and the shape loses on the people most likely to show up on day one. On a resume that is mostly duty statements with nothing after July 2023, the live product leads with "name the records you maintained and the reports you updated" and puts the three-year gap second, because the first fix never moves (`severity_check_v1.txt`: "Never change or question Fix 1"). On a resume that is already good, it still prints "One Critical Miss. Fix it first. It's the one most likely to cost you the interview." over an ask for an evaluation method on a 92. And the band's bottom rung, "Needs more context", covers everything from 0 to 69, so the person with ten things wrong and the person with three read the same word. Around the report, the product still forgets a returning person (the free counter defaults to 1, no link to the last report when signed in, pass holders see "Get your free report"), the wait can sit on "Sent" for two minutes with nothing capping it, and the saved report opens on two header rows and a blank band. None of that is a reason to stop; it is the list for the next wave, and most of it is small. The report is worth $29 for five more once the three holes below are closed; today I would not put the bad-resume case in front of a stranger and call it finished.

## 1. The job

From their side: **"I'm about to send this resume and I don't know what the person on the other end will think. Tell me what they'll trip on, and what to change tonight."**

Across a coffee, a sharp recruiter friend with the page in hand would, in roughly this order:

1. **Read the top and say what job this resume is for.** If the headline or objective doesn't say, they ask, and they tell you the reader won't.
2. **Scan titles and dates, and stop on anything that doesn't add up.** A role with no dates, a gap with nothing after it, a title the bullets don't earn. They say out loud which one a screener would use to pass.
3. **Find your best line and tell you to move it up.** "32 accounts, $2.4M ARR. That's the line. It's third."
4. **Point at the lines that say nothing.** "Responsible for managing customer relationships and renewals." "Assisted account managers with various tasks as needed." They ask what you actually did.
5. **Ask for the result that this kind of role lives on.** For a CSM: what happened to renewals. For a seller: quota. For a manager: team and budget. If it isn't there they ask whether you have it.
6. **Tell you to delete the filler.** "Team player with excellent communication skills" as a bullet. "Results-driven, detail-oriented professional seeking a challenging role." "References available upon request."
7. **Say what level you read as, and whether that matches the roles you want.** "This reads as a coordinator; you're applying for manager."
8. **Say whether the skills list helps or hurts.** "Microsoft Office, Communication, Leadership" hurts.
9. **If you brought the posting, say honestly whether this is a fit**, and name the two requirements the page doesn't show.
10. **Name the one thing to fix before you send it,** and say which of the rest can wait.
11. **Tell you where you stand,** in a sentence, without a number: "You're closer than you think" or "This isn't ready for the role you want."
12. **Tell you what they can't tell from the page:** layout, length as it prints, typos they'd notice on paper, and whether any of it is true.

That list is the standard below. Items 12 and part of 9 are limits any text-only read shares; the rest a report can do.

## 3. Is the Critical Miss actually critical?

Three tests per miss: would a recruiter stop here; is it a fact the person could add tonight; does the rewrite come from their facts.

**The sample (Jordan).** CM: "Introduced monthly renewal-risk reviews across the portfolio" with "Explain how you identified at-risk accounts and what follow-up you personally handled." A recruiter would not stop here. They would stop on the objective ("Results-driven, detail-oriented professional seeking a challenging role where I can utilize my skills.") and on "Team player with excellent communication skills" as a bullet, and only then wonder what the review process produced. It is a fact Jordan can add tonight, and the revised line is built only from the answers. Fix 2 (QBR topic) and Fix 3 ("Assisted account managers with various tasks as needed") are fair; Fix 3 is the one of the three that a recruiter would actually circle. The sample report carries no `critical` flags (it predates the severity check), so it never demonstrates the count heading the live product prints; a visitor judging the product by the sample never sees "Two Critical Misses".

**Daniel** (bar, real model). CM1 "Responsible for developing features for an internal inventory management web application using React and Spring Boot" → "Name a feature you developed and the inventory task it addressed." Right line, right ask, answerable tonight. CM2, added by the severity check: "In the Cardinal Health internship, add the size of the engineering team you worked on, if you can verify it." A recruiter would not pass a new grad for omitting intern team size. This is `no_evidence_latest_role` firing on a three-month internship, and the gate's own blind count call rated additions like it "not critical on their own" in 2 or 3 of 20 cases outside the key (`summary_severity_check_v1_2026-11-02.md`, "Padding outside the key isn't gated"). Its why, "Software engineering reviewers look for the scope of an intern's work", is a general claim about hiring the research library does not make.

**James.** CM1 "Launched ABM program targeting enterprise accounts" → add the activities or deliverables. A recruiter would ask that, but not first; first they would ask about March 2025 to now, and the report says "Your current freelance work is less detailed." The severity check made the freelance line CM2 ("Add the number of clients you served or a measurable project result"), which is the honest reading of the rule (a current line covers the time), and the question under it ("Which freelance assignment required a difficult tradeoff between strategy and execution support?") came from the general question list, not the fix (bar, finding 6; still live: `report-integrity.ts:353-367` moves any question whose words match a fix line onto that fix).

**Maria and Wei.** Both Critical Misses pass all three tests: the capstone finding, the ledger's operating scale against the posting. These are the product working.

**The 13 saved outputs.** Every first fix is a line edit (`summary_integrity_baseline_2026-11-01.md`: "Critical Miss is a line edit / structural: 13 / 0"); 7 reports flag a structural issue the Critical Miss skips; the Verdict and the Critical Miss disagree in 6; in 4 the Verdict raises no concern and the report still names one. Those are fair questions and they are not interview-costing problems, and the count lead says they are.

**The severity check's role.** It is finding real misses on weak resumes, not padding to a count: on the golden set it extended 6 of 23 reports, left 17 at one, and set "more than three" on none; 3 reports reached three. The under-count gate (every labeled bad resume gets at least 2 of its 3 problems) passed 8 of 8, the over-count gate (strong resumes not padded) 4 of 4. The order is the prompt's emit order plus the check's priority list, not a recruiter's.

- **Answer the question, copy the revised line.** One move, and it works: on the dev run the literal line read "Improved process across teams: the restock alerts feature for 40 warehouse staff." with "Every word is from your resume or your answers." and Copy enabled (`anon-report-answered.png`). - **"What to add" under a fix.** A list of questions with no field; the move is in the person's head.
- **"Keep this one."** No move; a reassurance.

## 7. The honest comparison

From their pages as `comparisons.tsx` quotes them, and ours, in the first five minutes:

- **Jobscan** gives a match rate against one posting and the list of missing terms, five scans a month free. For someone sending thirty applications a week, that list is more actionable than our report: it is a to-do list keyed to a specific posting, and it needs no answers from them. We give nothing per-posting unless they paste it, and then a paragraph and a Critical Miss, not a list. What we give that it can't: a read of whether the bullets hold up at all, the quoted line, and a rewrite that can't invent. Our compare page says this fairly ("a scanner that lists the terms you're missing saves real time").
- **Resume Worded** gives a score and a checklist with sample lines, instantly, and its checklist includes the things our report buries or skips: filler phrases, the summary, the skills section, length. A volume applicant with a weak resume gets more fixes per minute from it than from us, at a lower bar of truth (its sample lines are templates). We give one honest judgment and three fixes that come from their facts. For the bad resume, a checklist of ten is more useful on day one than three line edits, which is the economics problem in the holes below.

## What I would cut

- **"Full recruiter notes" as a section.** Section-by-section grades, "Other strengths", "Questions still open", "What to do next" and the role block repeat what the marked rows said or say things the friend would skip. Keep the next steps as the PDF's checklist and the one gap sentence from sketch 1; retire the rest. The report gets shorter and nothing a person acts on is lost.
- **"Questions only you can answer."** Five interview-style questions after the fixes. The three fix questions are the ones that produce a line; these produce a pause. Move them to the "After the offer" area or drop them.
- **Role fit without a posting.** "Company stage: Not clear from the resume alone" and "Industries: B2B SaaS, Logistics" are filler on the web and the PDF. Keep the one useful line ("Level suggested by the resume: …") and put it next to the band.
- **"How the score works."** It explains a number the first report hides. Fold its one honest sentence ("It doesn't predict interviews or offers.") under the band and drop the disclosure.
- **The first-name step at sign-in.** Nothing in the product uses the name where it matters; it is a tap between the code and the report.
- **The dead paths.** `HistorySidebar`, `VersionComparisonView`, `useReportSave.ts`, the LinkedIn mode and panels, the `lib/billing/generation-stream-failure.ts` copy nobody calls, and the `/dashboard` "Progress" link until Progress is a surface we would show a customer. Each one is a place copy drifts ("First impression", "Run Another", "Paid access") because nobody sees it.
- **Hide on every row.** A Critical Miss you can hide on this device is a feature for the one person in a hundred who disagrees, and it costs a control on every row of every phone screen. "Was this the right thing to fix first? No" already records the disagreement.
