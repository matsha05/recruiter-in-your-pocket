## 1. The replay harness

For every saved record under `docs/audits/2026-11-live-judgment/outputs/`, the harness sanitizes the resume the way the provider messages do, validates the model's first draft with `validateResumeModelPayload` and the saved posting, validates the saved repaired draft when the first is refused, and then merges the saved severity answer with `settleSeverityCheck`, exactly as `/api/resume-feedback` would. On the unchanged code the replay reproduces every decision in the saved records (`--check-saved`: 101 of 101 first-draft decisions and outcomes match, including the exact refusal messages). That is the before number. Every change below was measured against it with no model call. `tests/live-judgment-replay.test.ts` runs the replay in the `test:llm-contract` suite and asserts the after numbers, so a later validator change that re-refuses one of these runs fails a test.

What the saved records cannot show: a run whose first draft was refused in production carries only the one saved repair, so when a changed validator refuses a first draft that shipped before, the harness reports `regression_no_repair` rather than guessing. There are none after this wave. And the severity check's answer exists only for runs that shipped; for a run that never shipped before, the after report is the validated draft with no severity merge, which is what the harness reports.

## 3. The summary detector

`hasSummarySection` in `web/lib/llm/evidence-canonicalizer.ts` found no named heading, then called any non-contact line of 90 or more characters and 15 or more words before "EXPERIENCE" a summary, or in the first five lines when there was no experience heading. The student's "Relevant coursework: Introduction to Data Analysis, ..." line and the CV's "Ph.D., Molecular Biology, University of Cascadia, 2011. Dissertation: ..." line both qualified. The model, correctly, wrote that no summary exists; the canonicalizer refused that as a contradiction, the repair prompt repeated the instruction, the model repeated the truth, and the run died.

The rule now: a section is a section when the resume labels it (Summary, Professional Summary, Profile, Professional Profile, Objective, Career Objective) or when its shape is unambiguous: a prose paragraph in the opening, after the contact lines and before the first section heading of any kind (a known section name in any case, or a short all-capitals line after the first line, which is usually the person's name). A bullet, a labeled list ("Relevant coursework: ..."), a degree or credential line, or a dated line is content of another section, whatever its length. The COO resume's unlabeled opener ("Chief Operating Officer with twenty years in multi-site healthcare services. Scaled a dental services organization from 38 to 140 practices ...") is still a summary; a draft that calls it absent is still refused and repaired (b12 run 2, same as before).

The narrative audit's section-absence policy (`semantic-missing-policy.ts`) now judges "Section not present." and the exact sentinels by the same section detectors the canonicalizer uses (`source-sections.ts`), for Summary, Skills, Education and Work Experience, instead of by whether the word "section" appears in a positive bullet. The welder's "Welded pipe sections" had contradicted his Skills review's "Section not present." twice (both welder first drafts were refused for it; run 1 never shipped).

On replay: h2 ships 3 of 3 from the first draft, h4b ships 3 of 3 from the first draft, b03 ships 2 of 2 from the first draft. Nothing else is newly refused for a summary. Tests: `tests/judgment-wave-validators.test.ts` section 1 (the student resume, the CV, the COO opener, a labeled Summary, an all-capitals name with a headline above a paragraph, a long bullet under Experience, a long dated role line, a no-headings resume with an opener; and the welder's Skills both ways).

The CV had a second refusal behind the summary one: every draft's first fix was "Check the Drp1 publication entries against the original records and correct any repeated or inaccurate citation details", citing a title that appears in four different publication lines (an artifact of the generator that wrote this synthetic CV, which the model noticed, as a reader would). The one-fix-one-line rule called that excerpt ambiguous. A fix about repetition is about that repetition, so `ambiguousReportSourceLocators` now lets a fix whose text speaks of repeated or duplicate text cite the text it says is repeated; any other fix on a line that appears more than once is still ambiguous, and a rewrite never resolves a repeated line (tests, section 11).

## 6. The first fix moves

`web/lib/reports/severity-check.ts`: when the severity check returns a new `no_recent_work` or `title_level_mismatch` and the model's first fix is a line edit on a duty line, `mergeSeverityCheck` puts the structural miss first and the model's fix second; both are Critical Misses, and the model's fix is shipped exactly as written. `isDutyLineEdit` is the test: the fix cites a real bullet or sentence of the work history (not an absence marker, a date range, or a line in a summary, objective, profile, headline or title section), asks for detail on it rather than naming a structural problem (`structuralGapKind` finds none in its text), and the excerpt is a bullet or at least six words long. `no_evidence_latest_role`, `no_target_role`, `date_conflict` and `missing_dates` never lead; promotions never lead. `settleSeverityCheck` validates the merged report as before and now requires, through `keepsFirstFix`, that the validated report leads with the structural miss and keeps the model's fix second and unchanged; if the validator reorders or drops either, the merge does not ship and the report falls back as it did before. The outcome carries `led` (the kind that led, or null), logged with the rest of the severity outcome. The severity prompt is unchanged: "Never change or question Fix 1" still governs what the model writes; only the merge's order changed, in code.

## Proposal: not every good bullet needs a number

**What the saved outputs show.** The severity check's `no_evidence_latest_role` rule reads every latest role the same: "the latest role in the field this person wants shows no size and no result anywhere in its main work: no team, caseload, accounts, customers, budget or volume they personally run, and no measurable result." On the three student resumes it fired 6 of 6 times with asks a recruiter would not make of an intern: "add how many tickets or laptops you personally handled" (b10, both runs), "add the newsletter's audience size or a measurable result" (b11 run 1), "add the size of the Agile team you worked on or a measurable result of a feature you developed" (b39 run 1), "add how many users your work served" (b39 run 2). The resume prompt's own first fixes on the same resumes asked the better question: name the feature and what it let users do (b39), name the kinds of issues you handled (b10), name the information the dashboard tracked (h2). The head-to-head comparison (PR 64) saw the same preference for figures over evidence in the report's reading of a line about being the hiring contact for a chief security officer. And the unverifiable "Led a team of 40" was praised in 3 of 3 runs as "the size of your management responsibility" because the prompt tells the model to respect the ownership the resume states; a figure is treated as evidence when it has nothing behind it.

**The rule.** A bullet carries evidence when it names something a recruiter can hold and check: a decision, a named deliverable, a problem resolved, a named counterpart or audience, or a figure. A figure is one kind of evidence, not the standard. The ask for a number belongs where the role's defining measure is missing (a kitchen manager's food cost, a social worker's caseload, a dispatcher's drivers), not on every bullet, and not on an intern's. And a bare figure with nothing behind it ("Led a team of 40" over duty bullets) is a claim to question before it is a strength to praise.
