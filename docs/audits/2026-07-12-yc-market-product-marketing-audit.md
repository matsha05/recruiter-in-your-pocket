# RIYP: Market, Product, and Marketing Audit

**Date:** July 12, 2026
**Standard:** Would a strong YC partner believe this can become a company, not merely admire the website?

## Executive verdict

RIYP is now unusually well-art-directed for its category. The landing page, upload flow, and report feel authored, calm, and trustworthy. That earns attention. It does not yet make the company YC-ready.

The current product is strongest as a beautifully presented diagnosis and weakest at the moment after diagnosis: the report identifies missing context, but the user cannot answer the question, update the draft, rerun the resume, and see how the recruiter read changed. Until that loop exists and produces evidence of repeated use or referrals, RIYP remains a polished tool with a plausible insight—not yet a compounding product.

The most defensible positioning is:

> **For professionals whose experience is stronger than their resume makes it look.**
>
> Your experience is real. Your resume should make that obvious. See what a recruiter understands, where they have to guess, and what to fix first—before you send it.

The wedge is not resume writing, ATS scoring, or bullet rewriting. It is a calibrated recruiter first read: what lands, what creates doubt, and what deserves attention first, without inventing facts.

## Market reality

### Competitive set

**Resume Worded is the closest product threat.** Its current product scores resumes, flags specific lines, asks the user for missing factual context, drafts changes from that context, and asks the user to approve every edit. It claims more than five million users and charges $49 monthly, $99 quarterly, or $229 annually. RIYP cannot position itself as though competitors only provide generic scores.

**ChatGPT has commoditized generic resume assistance.** As of June 2026, all ChatGPT plans can upload or create a resume, tailor it to a role, and download a formatted version. “AI resume feedback” and “better bullet writing” are no longer products by themselves.

**Jobscan owns ATS anxiety and SEO distribution.** **Teal** and **Huntr** own the broader job-search workspace. **Rezi** and many builders compete on creation and formatting. RIYP should not become another job-search operating system or ATS oracle.

### Best initial customer

Mid-career knowledge workers—especially managers, career switchers, and people moving into greater scope—whose work is stronger than their resume presentation. They have real evidence to uncover, higher stakes, and enough professional complexity for a recruiter read to be meaningfully different from a rewrite prompt.

### Customer truth

The strongest recurring needs in current job-seeker discussions are:

- conflicting advice has created paralysis;
- users want an honest diagnosis, not another grade;
- ATS scores are treated as inconsistent and gameable;
- generic AI wording and invented details damage credibility;
- demands for quantified impact often push people toward fabrication;
- transparent billing and explicit data control are part of product trust;
- the desired outcome is confidence about what to change next.

The core job is therefore:

> End uncertainty and cause one truthful, useful revision.

## Product audit

### What is genuinely strong

1. **The report has an opinionated hierarchy.** “The read,” “what lands,” “where doubt creeps in,” and “fix these first” map to a recruiter's decision process better than a generic scorecard.
2. **Grounding is more than copy.** The backend rejects evidence excerpts that do not appear in the resume and blocks invented numerical specifics in drafts.
3. **The product is calm.** No-account entry, progressive job-description context, and finite recommendations reduce anxiety.
4. **The score has been demoted.** That aligns the interface with the company insight: judgment and prioritization matter more than a universal number.
5. **Research can support trust.** Its best role is explaining what is known, what is not, and how that changes practical advice.

### Product defects that matter to the company thesis

#### P0 — Complete the magic loop

The report currently asks the right factual question but does not let the user answer it in place. Build the full loop:

1. Ask for missing truthful context.
2. Update the working draft from the answer.
3. Let the user edit, reject, or copy it.
4. Invite a revised-resume rerun.
5. Compare the old and new first read—role impression, strongest evidence, remaining doubt, and priority—not just the score.

This is the product, not a feature. It turns static feedback into an action-and-learning system.

#### P0 — Make the public promise match the whole product

Move the line-rewrite interaction below the opening product proof. The hero should show the entire first read: perceived role and level, what lands, where the reader guesses, and the first move. A line-level change can then demonstrate one recommendation in practice.

#### P0 — Resolve trust contradictions

- Replace “First six seconds” with “First read” or “Opening read.”
- State the verified upload policy in plain language near the control: storage, deletion, model-training use, and relevant provider retention.
- Verify that actual vendor/project settings match every public privacy claim.

#### P0 — Instrument useful behavior

Add events for:

- recommendation opened;
- factual question answered or skipped;
- working draft edited;
- draft copied;
- recommendation accepted, dismissed, or marked not applicable;
- revised resume uploaded;
- rerun completed;
- first read changed;
- “I know what to change now” response;
- referral or shared report excerpt.

Raw uploads and score changes are not the north star. The leading indicator is whether the report ended uncertainty and caused a useful revision.

### Accessibility and responsive audit

Current strengths include semantic headings and landmarks, a skip link, accessible names, and no page-level horizontal overflow in the tested states. Risks include 40 px report tabs, clipped horizontal navigation without an obvious affordance, and small all-caps secondary labels. Screenshot inspection cannot prove color-contrast ratios, keyboard order, screen-reader announcements, reduced-motion behavior, or 200% zoom/reflow; those still require focused testing.

## Marketing audit

### The message to own

**Category:** recruiter first-read report.
**Enemy:** conflicting advice, invented specificity, and mysterious scores.
**Promise:** know what your resume communicates and what to fix first.
**Reason to believe:** evidence-linked judgments, truthful follow-up questions, recruiter benchmarking, and visible before/after first reads.
**Emotional outcome:** “I know what to change, and I am ready to send this.”

Recommended hero territory:

> **Your experience is real. Your resume should make that obvious.**
>
> See what a recruiter understands, where they have to guess, and what to fix first—before you send it.

Primary CTA: **Get my first read**
Secondary CTA: **Read a full sample**

Use charm to release tension, not to obscure the product. For example, “No doom wheel. No keyword confetti. Just the read.” can work once as supporting copy. It should not carry the proposition.

### Proof the site currently lacks

1. A blind recruiter benchmark against Resume Worded, Jobscan, and a strong ChatGPT prompt.
2. Real examples where a user answered a factual question and materially improved the first read.
3. Specific user testimony about clarity and action, not generic praise.
4. A transparent description of what RIYP does better and what it intentionally does not do.
5. A clear initial customer instead of universal job-seeker language.
6. A visible reason the founder understands recruiter judgment unusually well.
7. A paid signal; the current private-preview pricing surface explicitly says purchasing is unavailable.

### Distribution recommendation

Do not begin by trying to out-SEO Jobscan or by building Teal's feature set. Start with high-trust, high-intent channels:

- recruiter and career-coach referrals;
- alumni, professional, and career-transition communities;
- role-specific teardown content showing recruiter inference rather than generic tips;
- anonymized, shareable first-read excerpts;
- referral prompts after a user completes a meaningful revision.

The likely venture path is a focused consumer wedge followed by B2B2C distribution through coaches, outplacement firms, universities, workforce organizations, and career benefits—not accidental consumer subscription renewal.

## Business model hypothesis

The current $9 monthly and $79 lifetime options should be treated as hypotheses, not doctrine. Resume work is episodic, and a very low price can signal low value while a lifetime plan sells future usage before its cost is understood.

Test:

- a meaningful free first-read preview;
- a $9–12 one-time complete report;
- a $24–29 five-report pack or 30-day active-search pass;
- a quiet founding lifetime offer only if it helps early learning;
- partner pricing later, once the workflow and outcomes are repeatable.

## What would make this a credible YC application

YC will not accept RIYP because the interface looks expensive. Polish earns the first minute. The application becomes compelling when the team can show a non-obvious customer insight, a product people actively recommend, rapid learning, and a believable path to scale.

The likely interview objections are predictable: why this is not a prompt inside ChatGPT or a feature in Teal, who has paid, how many users returned with a revision, what improves as usage grows, what recruiter ground truth calibrates the advice, why the founder is uniquely qualified, and which acquisition channel repeats cheaply. The demo must answer those questions with a real resume and a visible transformation in under a minute.

### Evidence plan

1. **Blind quality benchmark:** 25–30 resumes, four or five working recruiters, and outputs from RIYP, Resume Worded, Jobscan, and a strong ChatGPT prompt. Score usefulness, prioritization, factual faithfulness, specificity, and recruiter agreement.
2. **Concierge pilot:** 25–50 target users. Observe the report session, collect the factual answers they can provide, watch what they edit or ignore, and measure revised-resume reruns and referrals.
3. **Founder-led distribution:** recruit through five to ten coaches/recruiters plus tightly defined professional communities.
4. **Learning corpus:** structure resume evidence, recruiter inference, user correction, accepted recommendation, rerun change, and reported outcome. That feedback loop—not the prompt or visual style—is the beginning of a moat.
5. **Application proof:** be able to state who loves it, where they came from, how many returned or referred someone, what surprised the team, and what changed in the product because of those observations.

### YC-readiness gate

Do not call the product YC-ready until RIYP can show:

- one painfully specific customer segment;
- the complete answer-to-rerun loop;
- at least 25 observed target users;
- a blind recruiter benchmark that shows a real advantage;
- a meaningful percentage who take action and return with a revision;
- several unprompted referrals or partner introductions;
- a pricing result based on behavior, not preference polling;
- a credible acquisition channel that does not depend on broad paid search.

## Ranked next 30 days

### Week 1 — Product truth

- Rename the six-second label.
- Replace the hero's dominant bullet rewrite with a whole-report proof surface.
- Lock the ICP and message.
- Put exact privacy behavior beside upload.
- Add action instrumentation.

### Week 2 — Completion loop

- Build answerable factual questions.
- Generate grounded draft updates from those answers.
- Add accept, edit, dismiss, and not-applicable states.
- Make revised-resume rerun the primary next action.
- Build first-read comparison.

### Week 3 — Evidence

- Run the recruiter benchmark.
- Start the concierge pilot.
- Replace generic marketing claims with benchmark and observed-user proof.

### Week 4 — Distribution and pricing

- Test one-time report versus active-search pass.
- Recruit coach/recruiter partners.
- Publish role-specific first-read teardowns.
- Measure referral behavior after a completed revision.
