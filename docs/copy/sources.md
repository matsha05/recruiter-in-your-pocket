# Where the copy system's methods come from

November 6, 2026. What RIYP takes from each source, and what it leaves.

| Source | What it is | What we take | What we leave |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Mailchimp content style guide, Voice and tone](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/) | Voice as four traits held in balance ("weird but not inappropriate, smart but not snobbish"); tone set by the reader's state of mind | The "we are, not" pairs in the voice chart; tone chosen by what the reader is going through, not by page type; "If you're unsure, keep a straight face" as the rule for asides | The mascot rules; their traits are theirs |
| [Shopify Polaris / app design guidelines, Voice and tone](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/design/content/voice-and-tone) | "Voice stays the same across your whole app. Tone changes with the situation." Headings in the form "Be real, but not too tough or overly familiar"; do/don't pairs per situation | The constant-voice, moving-tone split; do/don't pairs for each moment; "Words aren't always necessary" for everyday tasks | Their merchant-specific situations. The "Jenga rule" in our brief is not on this page; we keep the test (what can you take away before it falls apart) and credit it to our own review procedure |
| [Nielsen Norman Group, The four dimensions of tone of voice](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-of-voice-dimensions/) | Four sliding scales: formal/casual, serious/funny, respectful/irreverent, matter-of-fact/enthusiastic; tone words and anti-tone words; test with users | A position on each scale per moment in the tone table; anti-tone words as the "not" column | Their survey numbers; we measure our own copy with Matt's ratings |
| Torrey Podmajersky, *Strategic Writing for UX* (O'Reilly, 2nd ed.), and the public [VTEX voice chart](https://uxwriting.vtex.com/docs/principles/voice-chart/) built on it | A voice chart: traits as columns, and rows for concepts, vocabulary, verbosity, grammar, punctuation, capitalization | The row structure, so each trait says what it means for word choice, length and punctuation, not just a mood | Nothing; this is the most directly usable model |
| Intercom content design: [How the words you use build your product](https://medium.com/intercom-inside/how-the-words-you-use-build-your-product-6f5e558ce984) (Elizabeth McGuane; the page refused a direct fetch, summary from search) | Categorized every visible word in the product; the result became the product glossary | The inventory (`docs/copy/inventory/`) and one name per thing in the glossary | Their interview method; the founder is our naming authority |
| [Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) (WikiProject AI Cleanup) | A field guide to machine-written prose: negative parallelisms ("not just X, but Y", "It's not ..., it's ..."), the rule of three, copula avoidance ("serves as", "boasts"), superficial -ing analysis, vague attribution, puffed vocabulary | The structural tics in our list, named by pattern rather than by word, and the warning that these are signals, not proof | Wikipedia-specific signs (citations, edit summaries, markup) |
| [PostHog, writing-user-facing-copy skill](https://github.com/PostHog/posthog/blob/master/.agents/skills/writing-user-facing-copy/SKILL.md) | An agent skill every PostHog agent runs before touching a visible string: sentence case, no em dashes, no "not just X", no rule-of-three padding, and a section on "punchy or clever phrasing" with before/after pairs; errors always point to a next step | The gate's shape (run it before any string ships), the before/after format, the antithesis and weighty-heading tells, "if a line would feel out of place said plainly out loud to a colleague, rewrite it" | Their ban on all wit: RIYP's voice allows one dry aside where people pause, which PostHog's neutral voice does not |
| Zheng et al., [Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685) (2023) | Strong judges reach about 80% agreement with human preferences, near human-human agreement; position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases | A judge on a different model from the writer, no reward for length, order not mattering, and agreement measured rather than assumed | Pairwise mode: the voice judge scores strings absolutely, since there is no second candidate |
| Practitioner guides on calibrating judges ([LangChain](https://www.langchain.com/articles/llm-as-a-judge), [Cohen's kappa walk-through](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-08-31-calibrate-llm-judge-cohens-kappa/markdown)) | A human-labelled gold set; agreement as Cohen's kappa; the borderline cases, and the cases where judge and human disagree, become the few-shot examples; the prompt hash and model are a versioned contract | Matt's forty ratings as the gold set; kappa plus a list of every disagreement; disagreements fed back as exemplars; the prompt version and SHA in every run record | Their numeric thresholds; ours is set against Matt's labels |
| X and blog chatter on AI copy (summarized: [Daily Dot on the em dash fight](https://dailydot.com/em-dash-ai), [the binary-reframe tell](https://do-words-good.kit.com/posts/why-we-re-here-for-bringing-back-bold-imperfect-copywriting), founder landing-page complaints) | Sameness is the complaint: every landing page reads like the same agreeable copywriter; hedged claims that can never be wrong; the "it's not X, it's Y" line repeated in one paragraph | The swap test (could a competitor use this sentence unchanged) as the first question; read it aloud | Em dash panic as a detector; the house style already avoids dashes on its own grounds |
