November 6, 2026. Matt asked ChatGPT (with memory of his past conversations) to describe his writing and speech, using the prompt in this session. He pasted two replies. This is a condensed record of what they found and what the copy system takes from them. Both replies separate what they observed from what they inferred; that split is kept here. Personal anecdotes are left out.

## Where they disagree, or stop short

- **Referrals.** Both say they found no direct statement of Matt's view. His answers to this session's prompts (5, 13 and 14 in `matt-voice-2026-11-06.md`) are that direct statement: the resume is one variable, and a strong resume with no referral can still miss.
- **"Words he never uses."** Both decline to claim any; one notes he has used "leverage" in conversation. The tic list is about what reads as machine-made in product copy, not a list of words Matt never says.
- **Negotiation.** One reply quotes "EVERYONE negotiates. the only way to fail is to be a dick about it" and notes the overstatement; the guides already say "Most offers have some room."

## Guesses Matt endorsed

ChatGPT also wrote guesses at what Matt would say to a nervous job seeker. Matt picked these four as sounding like him (November 6). They are approximations, not quotes, and they are marked that way wherever they are used.

- "This is good. Why is it halfway down the page?"
- "If you don't have the number, don't make one up. Tell me what you do know."
- "Yeah, not hearing back sucks. We don't know why yet. I wouldn't take that as proof you're not qualified."
- "You don't need to fix every line today. Let's start with the part that's making your experience hard to understand."

Two notes for using them: "halfway down the page" is the literal sheet, which is fine; "the page" as a stand-in for the resume's content is still a coined word. And the product speaks as "we", so "I wouldn't take that" becomes "Don't take that" on the site.

## Matt's decisions from this read, November 6, 2026

- **Warmth yes, slang no.** His verdicts, contractions, "we", "that's fine", "it's common", "our bad". No "dude", "man", "hell yeah" or swearing anywhere on the site.
- **Errors in his words.** An error on our side owns it the way he would: "Our bad. Let's try that again." It still says what happened and whether it used a report, literally.
- **"We" is the candidate's side.** "We" means the candidate and us working on it ("We'll make sure the other lines are strong."). "I" stays in the founder note.
