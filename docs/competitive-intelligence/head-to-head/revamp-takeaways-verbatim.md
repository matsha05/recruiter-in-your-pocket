**The strongest pattern to borrow is the short path from a finding to a useful edit. The biggest opportunity for RIYP is making that edit more trustworthy—and making its effect understandable.**

I checked the local revamp too. Quoted originals, missing-fact questions, strengths, separate role fit, and version comparison already exist in the implementation. Much of this is about sharpening those features, rather than adding another set of tools. These recommendations come from our November 5 captures; they don’t establish conversion or hiring outcomes.

1. **Make substantive judgment outrank cosmetic optimization.**

   There’s a revealing example in your resume: **“300% reduction in time to hire.”** Jobscan included it among positive measurable results, and Resume Worded awarded its quantification check 10/10. A reduction of a positive duration cannot exceed 100%. That deserves clarification before repeated verbs or punctuation get attention. We cannot tell whether a locked check would have caught it. [Captured measurable results](/private/tmp/riyp-head-to-head/docs/competitive-intelligence/head-to-head/jobscan/measurable-results.txt)

   RIYP should distinguish credibility concerns, unclear responsibility, missing context, and optional polish. For that particular claim, the useful action is: “What was the hiring time before and after?” Then calculate and explain the supported wording.

   The same principle should protect useful qualitative evidence. Being the global hiring contact for a chief security officer can communicate responsibility without another percentage. **Don’t teach users that every good bullet needs a number.**

2. **Make answering a good question the central editing interaction.**

   Resume Worded’s free Magic Target suggestion added Greenhouse usage, intake work, and bar calibration to your Google experience. None was established by the resume. Its rationale treated the Google experience as making those additions believable. That is a concrete failure RIYP should test against. [Captured suggestion and errors](/private/tmp/riyp-head-to-head/docs/competitive-intelligence/head-to-head/resume-worded/errors.md)

   Rezi’s question about whether a keyword relates to someone’s experience is a useful starting point. RIYP should distinguish “I did this,” “I did related work,” and “I haven’t done this,” then ask only the relevant follow-up.

   Your current [FixCanvas](/Users/matsha05/Desktop/dev/recruiter-in-your-pocket/web/components/workspace/report/FixCanvas.tsx:255) already has missing-fact entry. I would give that interaction prominent product emphasis: the user supplies the missing fact, sees exactly how it changes the suggestion, and approves the result. An unconfirmed claim should never quietly enter finished wording.

3. **Keep evidence and action physically close together.**

   Resume Worded’s original-document view and Teal’s requirement popovers make it easier to understand what a recommendation refers to. Teal places the requirement, its source, and available actions together. That proximity is worth adopting. [Teal’s keyword interaction](/private/tmp/riyp-head-to-head/docs/competitive-intelligence/head-to-head/teal/keyword-1.png)

   For RIYP, selecting a fix should reveal its exact source passage, the concern, and the next action in the same reading area. On desktop, a contextual panel could work; on mobile, an inline excerpt is more useful than a miniature PDF.

   I would avoid copying the multiple independent scroll areas and dense toolbars. Also, quotation marks must mean a literal quotation: Jobscan sometimes displayed paraphrased snippets as quotes. Evidence inspection should reduce uncertainty.

4. **Turn role fit into a requirements assessment, with meaningful distinctions.**

   Teal usefully separates its general resume score from its job-match score. But its missing-keyword list treated “Full Lifecycle Recruiting” as absent even though your resume says “Full-cycle recruiting.” Resume Worded also surfaced “URL” from our posting header and “Banking” from the posting’s scam warning.

   RIYP should show the actual requirement beside the relevant resume evidence, distinguishing:

   | Finding | Appropriate next action |
   |---|---|
   | Experience clearly demonstrated | Preserve it; consider placement |
   | Related experience, but wording or scope is unclear | Clarify the connection |
   | Experience not established in the resume | Ask whether the candidate has it |

   Company background, benefits, application instructions, and provenance headers should not become qualification gaps. The existing separate Role fit section is the right foundation. **Adding a posting should help explain relevance without making the underlying resume assessment mysterious.**

5. **Preserve the complete useful first report.**

   Teal showed 16 Analyzer issues while locking all 16 detailed recommendations. Kickresume gave an 80 score and identified Content as the weakest category, but its actionable details were locked. Those screens demonstrated that an assessment existed without letting us evaluate much of its usefulness. [Teal capture](/private/tmp/riyp-head-to-head/docs/competitive-intelligence/head-to-head/teal/headline.md), [Kickresume capture](/private/tmp/riyp-head-to-head/docs/competitive-intelligence/head-to-head/kickresume/headline.md)

   RIYP’s free report should deliver a complete experience: the judgment, its evidence, what to preserve, and a usable next step. I would protect that promise through the revamp.

   Paid value can come from continued use and subsequent reviews. I would avoid blurred criticism, unexplained issue counts, or withholding the explanation behind the Critical Miss. Whether a more generous first experience converts better needs testing; this audit alone cannot prove it.

6. **Use positive feedback to prevent unnecessary editing.**

   Resume Worded’s opening usefully identified your security-hiring bullet as an example of specificity worth carrying elsewhere. That is more helpful than generic encouragement.

   RIYP’s existing “Keep these” section should tell users precisely what to preserve and why: the scope is clear, the result is supported, the responsibility is unusually relevant, or the wording is already concise. It should sometimes say that a line needs no change.

   That also gives the product a credible stopping point. Users should be able to finish with a stronger document, rather than feel compelled to keep rewriting until a gauge reaches an arbitrary target.

7. **Keep the uploaded document, account facts, and proposed revision distinct.**

   In our clean Teal account, importing the PDF introduced the account email and onboarding target title. Rezi added summary and skills sections and misread characters in company names and levels. Kickresume scored a rebuilt representation. [Teal import differences](/private/tmp/riyp-head-to-head/docs/competitive-intelligence/head-to-head/teal/errors.md), [Rezi import differences](/private/tmp/riyp-head-to-head/docs/competitive-intelligence/head-to-head/rezi/errors.md)

   RIYP should always make it clear which resume and posting a report reviewed. Account information should not silently enter the resume. Proposed changes should remain distinguishable from the original.

   When extraction is uncertain, give the user a focused correction route. A parser failing to recognize an education heading should not become an authoritative statement that the candidate omitted education. Useful correction choices would include “already present,” “wrong job,” “incorrect quotation,” and “not relevant.”

8. **Make version comparison explain which concern was resolved.**

   Resume Worded prominently displayed a potential score of 92. Rezi’s observed score moved from 56 to 96 while several category scores changed without manual resume edits; we could not isolate the cause of that movement. These experiences show how easily progress becomes hard to interpret.

   RIYP already has comparison components. The valuable next step is connecting a previous concern to the new evidence that addresses it:

   > Previously, your responsibility for this result was unclear. The revised line identifies the decision you owned. The outcome still needs confirmation.

   The current [ReadComparison](/Users/matsha05/Desktop/dev/recruiter-in-your-pocket/web/components/workspace/report/ReadComparison.tsx:26) detects changed text; changed wording alone does not establish improvement. **Track resolved concerns, remaining questions, and newly introduced claims alongside any score movement.** This would make returning for another report substantially more useful.

9. **Design the entire route to useful advice—including interruptions.**

   Jobscan’s onboarding led us through job matching before another upload in the scanner. Resume Worded inserted introductions and tours. Teal retained a spinner until a normal reload. These were session observations, not reliable speed rankings.

   RIYP should retain the upload and posting through authentication, make tours optional, preserve a pending report through refresh, and explain known access limits before the user invests unnecessary effort.

   Our own RIYP attempts reached exhausted-free-report gates. The local revamp contains entitlement messaging, but the actual production path still needs separate verification. Measure when someone reaches and understands a useful recommendation—not only when generation finishes.

10. **Borrow workflow continuity; be selective about expanding the suite.**

   Competitors increasingly surround resume review with builders, trackers, cover letters, job discovery, and interview tools. Their current public navigation makes that breadth explicit. [Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/), [Teal](https://www.tealhq.com/)

   The useful lesson is keeping the same document and context available as someone moves between related tasks. Feature breadth itself is not evidence that RIYP needs all those products.

   I would defer a full builder, auto-apply system, template marketplace, and general career dashboard unless repeated user behavior exposes a specific unmet need. For this revamp, the coherent experience is: understand the recruiter’s concern, supply missing evidence, inspect an honest revision, and see whether it addressed the concern.

If I were setting the remaining revamp priorities, I’d put **credibility-aware prioritization, a polished clarification-and-edit interaction, and evidence-based version comparison** first. Those build on what is already underway and would give users concrete reasons to trust and return to RIYP.
