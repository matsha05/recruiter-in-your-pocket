Common questions
Answered by real recruiters and hiring managers
Why should personal details be removed from your resume?

Your resume should only include information that's relevant to your ability to do the job: this includes your professional achievements, educational qualifications, or skills.

Things like your date of birth, marital status, gender or nationality are not relevant to your ability to do the job. They are personal details and are not factors that hiring managers consider when reviewing your resume.

Including these details can show a lack of professional judgment and can make it seem like you are unaware of professional norms. This could lead to you to being rejected.

Additionally, including private information like your Passport Number or Social Security Number is also a mistake. Not only is it not relevant to your application, it could be misused if you share and post your resume on multiple job sites or publicly.

The only rare exception to these rules is if you're in a country that has different professional requirements or if a company has explicitly asked for some personal information to be included on your resume. Even then, it's likely you should include this only on your application form, and not on your resume, which is specifically for professional achievements.

What contact details should I include?

It's important that potential employers and recruiters can easily reach you, so ensure you've included your email address at the bare minimum. You can optionally include mobile number too. While you should not include your full physical address, recruiters find it easier to determine your eligibility if you include your current city on your resume. This is particularly useful if your previous job locations were in a different city to the job you're now applying for.

I'm applying outside the US — do these rules still apply?

Yes, in most cases. The only rare exception to these rules is if you're in a country that has different professional requirements or if a company has explicitly asked for some personal information to be included on your resume. Even then, it's likely you should include this only on your application form, and not on your resume, which is specifically for professional achievements.

Should I include a photo on my resume?

In most cases, no. In the US, UK, Canada and Australia, many employers have policies against reviewing resumes with photos, because a photo opens them up to discrimination claims. Some will discard the resume rather than take the risk.

Photos are more common in parts of Europe, the Middle East and Asia, but they are rarely required there either, and strong candidates typically leave them off. Unless an employer has specifically asked for one, you are safe without it.

Either way, a photo doesn't help a hiring manager judge whether you can do the job. Your experience does.

Does my graduation year give away my age?

It can, and for most people that's fine — recruiters expect to see when you graduated, and leaving it off can look like you're hiding something. If you have more than 15–20 years of experience and you're concerned about age bias, it's acceptable to drop the graduation year from your education section and let your recent experience speak for itself.

Don't remove the dates from your work experience, though. Recruiters need them to follow your career timeline.
