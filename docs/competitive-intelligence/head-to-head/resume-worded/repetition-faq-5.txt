Common questions
Answered by real recruiters and hiring managers
Why is repetition bad?

Repetition often reduces your resume's impact, making it less compelling and harder for your achievements to stand out.

If you've used the exact same action verbs or phrases several times over in your resume, it's usually a sign that you're showing the same skill set too often.

Repetition of action verbs

You shouldn't use the same action verb more than two times on your resume. For alternative action verbs, see the image on the right of the question, the alternatives below, or go to the Action Verbs section.

Repetition of phrases

Similarly, try to avoid reusing specific phrases multiple times on your resume.

We see this mistake pretty often, where job seekers want to emphasize a certain skill and describe it in the exact same way in multiple parts of their resume (e.g. their work experience and their summary). Instead, try to vary up the language even when describing similar skills - this helps your resume's bullet points stand out.

Is it okay to repeat a company, product or skill name?

Yes. Names that can't be described another way — a company, a product, a tool like Salesforce or Python — are fine to repeat, and recruiters expect to see them. The repetition we flag is in your own wording: the verbs and phrases you chose. If a flagged phrase is actually a name, keep it and flag the finding so it stops counting against your score.

What if my roles really were similar?

They often are, and that is exactly when varied language matters most. Even when describing similar skills, use different verbs and phrases, and make each bullet point to a different project, metric or result. The space on your resume is limited, so two bullets that say the same thing cost you one that could show something new.

What does a fix look like?
BEFORE
• Coordinated across engineering, operations, and vendor teams to deliver high-availability systems
AFTER
