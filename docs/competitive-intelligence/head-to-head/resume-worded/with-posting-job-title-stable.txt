  JOB TITLE MISSING
   Needs review. We did not find the job title, Technical Recruiter, on your resume.

Especially if you have held a similar role before, try to include the same job title on your resume to improve your resume's success rate. Read the below for details and examples of how to do this.

WHY IS THIS IMPORTANT?
Recruiters initially spend just a few seconds reviewing your resume to evaluate whether or not you are a good fit for the role. Especially if a role requires some experience, recruiters quickly scan your previous role titles to make sure you have relevant experience.

Similarly, Applicant Tracking Systems (i.e. automated resume screeners) often place significant weight on this too - some companies may auto-reject your application if you haven't held relevant or similar titles in the past.

I'VE HELD A SIMILAR JOB IN THE PAST, BUT IT'S NOT THE EXACT JOB TITLE. WHAT SHOULD I DO?

Where possible, you want to be including the exact job title into your resume. For example, if you have held, or currently work in, a similar position, consider adding the exact job title somewhere in your resume. This improves your chances of getting past the resume screening stage.

Example: If you are applying to a 'Business Analyst' job...

If your job title is 'Analyst': Consider renaming it to 'Business Analyst'.
If your job title is 'Data Analyst': Consider using the combined 'Data Analyst / Business Analyst' as your job title.
If your job title is 'Business Ninja': Use 'Business Analyst'. Avoid company-specific buzzwords and terminology (e.g. 'Ninja'), which mean little to an external recruiter or hiring manager.
Using similar job titles where appropriate is not dishonest. Companies have different naming conventions, especially across industries. If you have held a similar role but it is named differently at the company you're applying to, it's important to make it easier for a recruiter to understand that.

That said, if you have never worked in a similar role, do not lie and say you have. Remember, the resume is just the first phase of the recruitment process - don't risk it.


I'M CHANGING CAREERS AND HAVE NEVER HELD A SIMILAR ROLE IN THE PAST. WHAT SHOULD I DO?

Consider adding a very short summary at the start of your resume outlining what you are looking for. For example, if you are a project manager and you want to move into a marketing role, you could use something like:

Project management and marketing professional with 5 years of experience seeking a position as a marketing lead in consumer software. Managed launches of two large-scale projects including...

If you have never worked in a similar role, please do not lie and say you have. Try to instead demonstrate the skills you do have through your accomplishments.
