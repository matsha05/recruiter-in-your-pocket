## Unsupported experience added in a suggested rewrite

Magic Target Suggestion #1 rewrites the Google bullet to say “managing candidates end-to-end in Greenhouse (applicant tracking system) while partnering with hiring managers on intake and bar calibration.” The input only says “Full-cycle recruiting from 2018-2020 across a variety of roles from sales, product, operations and engineering.” It does not claim Greenhouse use, intake work, or bar calibration. The tool's rationale explicitly says “your Google experience makes them believable.” Those additions are unsupported by the supplied resume; this capture cannot establish whether they are true in real life. This is a factual invention in proposed text, not a rewrite accepted into the input file. Suggestions 2–6 remained locked and were not evaluated.

## Posting-keyword relevance limitations

The exact title “Technical Recruiter” is absent from the resume, so that literal missing-title finding is accurate. The missing-keyword list also includes **URL**, which comes from the required provenance header of the shared posting file, and **Banking**, which appears in the posting's scam-warning text. These are not recruiting qualifications. Other listed words such as **Neurons**, **Concrete**, **Preferences**, **Physics**, and **Biology** occur in company research background/publication names, rather than the role's recruiting qualifications. The tool treated them as missing “keywords and skills.” This is an observed context/relevance issue, not an invented resume quotation. The shared full posting was deliberately left unchanged across tools; no terms were excluded from scoring.

## Redaction effect, not a tool error

“We couldn’t find your email address” is expected: the input deliberately contains `[EMAIL] / [PHONE]`. The contact-details check therefore measures the redacted test input. It should not be interpreted as a defect in the unredacted original resume.

## Rule inconsistency observed

The repetition panel says, “You shouldn’t use the same action verb more than two times on your resume,” but includes “Spearheaded ×2” among the repeated verbs. Two uses do not exceed the stated threshold. The count itself matches the resume; the apparent inconsistency is between the displayed rule and the flagged count.

## Initial access friction

The anonymous signup flow required a human-verification check. After a manual attempt it reported, “Verification could not finish. Your details are still here. Try signing up again, or use the existing Google signup option.” A retry again requested the check. The user later completed authentication separately and the free baseline report was captured. This is access friction, not a resume-analysis error.
