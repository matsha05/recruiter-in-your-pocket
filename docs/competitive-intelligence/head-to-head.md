# Head-to-head: one resume through every tool, November 5, 2026

The compare pages say we have not run the other tools and ours on the same resume. On November 5 Matt did, through Codex, following [head-to-head-protocol.md](head-to-head-protocol.md). One three-page resume for a senior technical recruiter, contact details replaced, and one real posting for a Technical Recruiter role at an AI lab, through the free tiers of Jobscan, Resume Worded, Teal, Rezi and Kickresume, and through our own product on production. The evidence packet (every screenshot, every recommendation transcribed and tagged, the timings and the errors checked against the input) is [PR 63](https://github.com/matsha05/recruiter-in-your-pocket/pull/63). This file is the comparison written from it. No winner is declared; it says what each tool said.

**The first finding was about us.** Every competitor returned something. Recruiter in Your Pocket returned nothing, twice: signed out, the free-report gate said the report "on this device or network has been used"; signed in to the founder's own account, the free report had already been spent. Both gates were working as designed. A five-report pass was then granted to the account directly in the database (no Stripe, so the purchase and refund rehearsal in the launch program stays open), and the same resume and posting went through ours twice, with the posting and without. Those two runs fill the sixth column below. Later the same day the founder ran his unredacted resume a third time from a browser; the three runs landed at 84, 86 and 84 on the hidden score.

## The inputs

- One resume, unchanged, the same bytes for every upload. Mid-career, three pages, tech recruiting leadership across several well-known employers, numbers in most bullets, no summary section, education and certification in one combined section. Phone and email replaced by placeholders, which every tool then flagged as missing; those flags are not counted as errors.
- One posting, pasted in full including the company boilerplate, which matters below.
- Where a tool scores, it was run with and without the posting. Teal, Rezi and Kickresume rebuild the resume into their own template before scoring, so their numbers describe their imported copy, not the file.

The packet is kept out of this folder on purpose: the resume carries the founder's name and employers, and the site is anonymous by design. The quotes below are chosen so that nothing identifies the person.

## What each tool gave in the first five minutes

**Jobscan** gave the most, fastest: a match rate against the posting in under a minute, with specific findings (the posting's exact title is absent, which is true and useful) mixed with generic ATS folklore (an address to "validate your location"). It also got the resume wrong in two places a recruiter never would: it could not find the education section because the heading has three words in it, and it congratulated the person on a summary that doesn't exist. Its best material is keyword matching, which is what it sells. Without a posting it refuses to run at all.

**Resume Worded** came closest to a recruiter's read: its first three comments quote lines from the resume and say what's missing from each ("there isn't a single number in this line"). That is the shape of our Critical Miss. Around those three sit 78 generic items and a tour. Its serious miss is in the rewrite: offered one Magic Target suggestion, it wrote experience the person never claimed and justified it by the employer's reputation. That is the exact thing "only from your facts" exists to prevent. Then the email.

**Teal** scored the resume 45% and locked all sixteen reasons. With the posting it listed five missing phrases, one of which the resume contains in different words. On the free tier it is a score and a lock.

**Rezi** moved the same resume from 56 to 96 by adding the posting, after rebuilding it, adding a summary and skills the person didn't write, and misreading a company name. Its readable advice is format and verb rules. The number is not a read of the resume.

**Kickresume** rebuilt the resume into its template, scored the rebuild 80, and locked everything else. The protocol calls it "builder only," and that held.

**Recruiter in Your Pocket** read the resume rather than the posting's word list. Both runs led the Verdict with the resume's own figures, circled one line as the Critical Miss with a question only the person can answer, and questioned the "300% reduction in time to hire" as a number that cannot describe a reduction in elapsed time, which no competitor caught (Jobscan listed it among positive "measurable results"). Nothing was misread or invented in the quoted text. Two things it did worse than it should: the Critical Miss changed between the two runs (a search-specifics question with the posting, a funnel-contribution question without) while the 300% problem, the one a recruiter would raise first, sat second or third both times; and the band moved from "Mostly clear" to "Clear and specific" on the same file with only the posting removed. The job-match score of 75 lived inside the expanded notes. The rewrite flow was not exercised, so "only from your facts" remains a design rule here, not a tested result.

## What this says about us, which is the point

1. **Our gate blocked the one person most likely to try it.** The anonymous free-report ledger blocks by device or network, and the signed-in account had used its free report. Both are working as designed, and both meant the first attempt could not include us. The pass was granted by hand; the launch program (`docs/launch-readiness/80-go-no-go-program.md`) still requires a real purchase and refund by the founder before charging strangers.
2. **The competitors' errors are our argument, and so far we don't make them.** A missed section, an invented summary, a rewrite that adds experience: those are the failures the research articles warn about, and on this resume ours made none of them. The live-judgment run ([2026-11-live-judgment.md](../audits/2026-11-live-judgment.md)) shows where we fail instead: the validator refuses the model's honest answer too often, and one detector mistakes a long line for a summary. Different failure, same category. "We don't invent" holds on this resume; it is still untested on the rewrite.
3. **Specific beats generic, and we are the only tool that is mostly specific.** The best competitor was 10 specific items in 110. Ours was 24 of 44 with the posting and 25 of 37 without. That is the gap to own, and the reason the first screen should be the circled line and nothing else.
4. **Priority is our weak spot, not judgment.** Both of our runs found the impossible percentage and both ranked it behind a request for more detail on an already strong line. Codex's takeaways say the same thing from the outside: make every "fix first" surface agree, show the whole quoted bullet with its employer, say which entitlement a button spends, and drop the interview-cost claim where the band doesn't support it. All four are already on the review's list (`docs/audits/2026-11-report-and-signed-in-review.md`, gaps 1, 3, 4 and the quotation fault from the live run).
5. **The follow-up email is a trust choice.** A named expert, your employers in the subject, "companies are actively seeking leaders with your experience": that is the register the brand system calls hype. We send nothing after a report but the report. The Resume Worded page now says so, dated.
