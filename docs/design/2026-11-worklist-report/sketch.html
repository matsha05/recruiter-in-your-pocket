What I'd tell you

The resume says manager and the experience says intern, and that mismatch is the first thing a recruiter sees. It isn't fatal; it's a credibility problem on line one, and everything after it gets read with a little suspicion. The fix is cheap: say what you are. A year of credit union marketing straight out of an economics degree is a fine story for a coordinator role. It just isn't the story the headline tells.

Below that, the marketing internship is three lines that could belong to anyone. The research job, by contrast, is the best-written thing on the page: 1,200 farms, Stata, 15 summaries. Nobody has to guess what you did. The marketing bullets need the same treatment, and you already know how to write it. Which accounts, how many members, which event. If a number moved, say so. If it didn't, say what you made.

What's the same in both
