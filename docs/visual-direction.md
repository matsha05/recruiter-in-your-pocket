## 1. Visual Thesis

RIYP should look like a premium decision instrument with a human expert behind it.

The visual language should communicate:
- first-pass judgment
- exactness
- composure
- control
- relief through clarity

The product should feel:
- headline-led
- crisp
- slightly denser than a typical marketing site
- high-contrast
- sparse with color but not colorless
- specific in its rhythm and hierarchy

The product should not feel:
- editorial by default
- ornamental
- trendy
- soft and boutique
- dashboard-busy
- startup-performative

## 2. Approved Direction

### Direction: Composed Bold

This is the approved visual direction for RIYP.

#### Mood

- sharp
- calm
- premium
- high-control
- human without coziness

#### Core Image

Think:
- a recruiter briefing
- a controlled transformation
- a decisive report object
- a tool used by someone with standards

Not:
- a magazine feature
- a creative portfolio
- a paper-texture lifestyle brand
- a generic productivity dashboard

#### What It Solves

- makes the brand feel more ownable
- keeps warmth without drifting into softness
- lets typography and composition carry the distinction
- gives teal more presence without turning it into wallpaper

#### Biggest Risk

If pushed too far, it can become cold or over-corporate.

The counterbalance is not warmth everywhere.
It is human copy, precise motion, and selective proof-surface softness.

## 3. Alternate Directions Rejected

### Light Editorial Default

Rejected because it:
- diffuses authority
- softens the homepage tension
- turns proof styling into the master identity

### System-Heavy Product Cool

Rejected because it:
- genericizes the brand
- loses human presence
- makes RIYP feel like “good B2B software” instead of a recruiter-specific instrument

## 4. Color Direction

### 4.1 The Big Decision

Keep teal visible.
Cool down the world around it.
Use paper only where proof needs it.

### 4.2 Approved Palette Family

The palette family is `Ink + Mineral`.

Primary roles:
- Ink: primary type and high-authority contrast
- Mineral: cooled neutral surfaces, frames, and dividers
- Quiet white: raised surfaces and report cards
- Teal: active identity, proof emphasis, key states
- Aged brass: rare premium highlight
- Paper: contextual proof-surface warmth only

### 4.3 Palette Behavior

#### Core Neutrals

These should do most of the work.

- neutrals should lean slate and mineral, not beige
- section shifts should feel tonal, not decorative
- dark surfaces should feel controlled, not luxury-black

#### Brand Accent

Teal remains the primary brand signal.

It should mean:
- action
- focus
- exactness
- evidence emphasis
- active state

Teal should be clearly visible in:
- the hero composition
- primary CTA states
- evidence cues
- section accents

Teal should not be used as:
- a full-site wash
- decorative glow
- a replacement for layout hierarchy

#### Premium Accent

Use aged brass only for:
- premium plan emphasis
- upgrade moments
- selective elevated metadata

Never use brass as the second main brand color.

#### Warm Paper

Use paper for:
- research
- methodology
- select proof or annotation moments

Do not use paper as the homepage climate or shared-chrome default.

### 4.4 Suggested Usage Ratio

For core marketing surfaces:
- 68% cool neutrals
- 20% white and raised surfaces
- 8% teal emphasis
- 3% paper support moments
- 1% brass premium note

For research and methodology:
- 50% neutral
- 28% paper
- 15% white
- 6% teal
- 1% brass

For workspace and reports:
- 76% neutral
- 15% white
- 7% teal
- 2% semantic or premium color

### 4.5 Surface Recipes

| Surface | Base | Secondary | Accent | Overall Feel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Homepage hero | cool off-white + ink | quiet white artifact | visible teal | sharp, authored, high-control |
| Mechanism / transformation | mineral mist + white | near-ink detail bands | teal + semantic warning | exact, legible, product-first |
| Trust + pricing | neutral white + mist | subtle raised planes | teal + brass only where earned | calm, adult, matter-of-fact |
| Workspace | neutral field | raised white modules | teal in active states | precise, denser, tool-like |
| Report | white + controlled borders | restrained evidence bands | teal + semantic warning | fair, legible, exact |
| Research | paper + neutral text | white figures | teal for evidence emphasis | editorial proof, not main brand |

### 4.6 Colors To Explicitly Avoid

- violet-to-blue gradients
- bright cobalt as a peer accent
- emerald as a parallel brand signal
- pink or rose warmth washes
- black-only luxury contrast

## 5. Typography Direction

### 5.1 Main Rule

Typography must do more authority work than effects, texture, or color.

### 5.2 Font Role Discipline

#### Sentient

Use for:
- decisive headlines
- verdicts
- section-defining moments
- exact callouts where judgment matters

Do not use for:
- long paragraphs
- dense UI
- repetitive labels
- decorative brand theater

Sentient should feel like authority entering the room.

#### Satoshi

Use for:
- body copy
- metadata
- controls
- pricing
- labels
- evidence rows
- trust copy

Satoshi should feel like the operating system of the product.

### 5.3 Type Mood

The type system should feel:
- literate, but not literary
- premium, but not luxury-fashion
- exact, but not sterile

### 5.4 Hierarchy Rules

- Headlines should carry the brand’s character
- Supporting copy should stay direct and highly readable
- Labels should feel evidentiary, not decorative
- Numbers should feel clean and deliberate

### 5.5 Working Sizes

| Surface | Role | Suggested Size / Line Height | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero headline | display | 54/52 to 68/64 | compressed, high-authority |
| Hero support | body | 18/29 to 20/31 | keep it brief |
| Section title | display | 36/36 to 48/46 | strong, not sprawling |
| Section body | body | 16/26 to 18/28 | one thought at a time |
| Product UI body | body | 14/20 to 14/22 | efficient and clear |
| Metadata | mono/sans | 11/16 to 12/18 | crisp evidence language |
| Evidence lines | body | 13/20 to 14/22 | fast scanning matters |

### 5.6 Type As Signature

World-class RIYP should be recognizable through:
- a decisive serif headline
- sharp supporting sans
- compact evidence labels
- visible contrast between statement and proof

## 6. Layout And Composition

### 6.1 Core Posture

The layout should feel structured, not boxed in.

Prefer:
- one dominant composition in viewport one
- split layouts
- asymmetry with control
- long rails
- varied section rhythm

Avoid:
- equal-weight card farms
- bento grids
- nested card stacks
- center-aligned sameness

### 6.2 First Viewport Rule

The first viewport must read as one composition.

It should contain:
- one dominant headline
- one supporting proof object
- one main action
- one narrow proof or trust cue

The artifact should support the headline, not compete with it.

### 6.3 Surface Recipes

#### Recipe A: Precision Instrument

Use for:
- hero artifact
- report previews
- key product modules

Ingredients:
- quiet white surface
- crisp border
- cool shadows
- compact labels
- one accent entry point

#### Recipe B: Transformation Stage

Use for:
- before / after moments
- line-level proof
- rewrite demonstrations

Ingredients:
- one weak line
- one stronger line
- one exact reason
- one clear visual transition

#### Recipe C: Evidence Rail

Use for:
- recruiter logic
- signal summaries
- methodology support

Ingredients:
- open row
- limited framing
- compact labels
- small proof marks
- minimal accent use

#### Recipe D: Proof Surface

Use for:
- research
- methodology
- citation-heavy moments

Ingredients:
- paper support tone
- open article spacing
- calm labels
- restrained annotation

## 7. Motion Direction

### 7.1 Motion Mood

Motion should feel polished and lively, not static, but also never theatrical.

Use motion for:
- sequencing
- transformation
- state clarity
- artifact realism

Avoid:
- constant shimmer
- bounce
- flourish without meaning

### 7.2 Signature Motion Moments

- artifact reveal
- line transformation
- signal scoring or weighting
- proof-strip entrances

### 7.3 Reduced Motion

Every signature motion moment must degrade cleanly to static hierarchy.

## 8. Imagery Direction

### 8.1 Expert Presence

Selective founder or expert presence is encouraged.

The role of imagery is:
- point of view
- credibility
- humanity

It is not:
- lifestyle filler
- candidate stock imagery
- brand wallpaper

### 8.2 Default Treatment

Approved default:
- real-first photography
- monochrome hero treatment
- warm authority expression
- controlled natural art direction

Generated treatments are allowed only as subtle extensions of real source photography.

### 8.3 Where Imagery Belongs

Use imagery most naturally in:
- hero support systems
- trust sections
- campaign surfaces

Do not make photography the main homepage object.

## 9. Decision Rules

If a designer is unsure, default to:

1. Clarity of promise
2. Headline-led composition
3. Precision-instrument surface language
4. Disciplined visible teal
5. Paper only where proof calls for it
- long paragraphs
- dense UI
- repetitive labels
- over-decorated brand theater

Sentient should feel like judgment, not wallpaper.

#### Satoshi

Use for:
- body copy
- controls
- labels
- pricing
- metadata
- evidence rows
- all practical interface work

Satoshi should feel like the engine of the product.

### 5.3 Type Mood

The type system should feel:
- literate, but not literary
- premium, but not luxury-fashion
- exact, but not mechanical

### 5.4 Scale Guidance

#### Marketing

- H1: large, compressed, 2 to 4 lines max
- Body: one sentence at a time
- Metadata and proof labels: compact and crisp

#### Product

- default body should feel slightly denser than marketing
- many surfaces should run comfortably at a 14px workhorse size
- evidence and controls should scan fast

#### Report

- headings should guide reading order clearly
- labels should feel like evidence tags, not UI garnish
- numbers should be clean and tabular

### 5.4a Recommended Working Sizes

These are suggested defaults for new work where the current screen does not already establish a better local scale.

| Surface | Role | Suggested Size / Line Height | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marketing | hero body | 18/28 to 20/30 | keep paragraphs brief |
| Marketing | section body | 16/26 to 18/28 | should read quickly |
| Product | default UI body | 14/20 to 14/22 | should feel efficient |
| Product | secondary metadata | 12/16 to 12/18 | labels, timestamps, support text |
| Report | evidence row body | 13/20 to 14/22 | fast scanning matters more than elegance |
| Report | section intro | 15/24 to 16/24 | calm and direct |
| Research | article body | 17/30 to 18/32 | can breathe more than product |

### 5.4b Line Length Rules

- homepage supporting copy: 40 to 55 characters per line ideal
- app surfaces: 55 to 75 characters per line ideal
- research: 60 to 78 characters per line ideal

If paragraphs run wider than that, the brand starts feeling more generic and less authored.

### 5.5 Tracking And Weight

Recommended posture:
- fewer weights overall
- slightly tighter headline tracking
- strong weight contrast between labels and body
- avoid semibold everywhere

Common failure mode:
- serif headline
- medium body
- medium labels
- medium buttons

That creates sameness and weakens hierarchy.

### 5.6 Type As Brand Signal

World-class RIYP should be recognizable through:
- a decisive serif moment
- clean supporting sans
- exact evidence labels
- reduced typographic noise

## 6. Layout And Composition

### 6.1 Core Posture

The layout should feel structured, not boxed in.

Preferred:
- rails
- long rows
- split compositions
- one dominant object
- open section rhythm

Avoid:
- equal-weight card farms
- too many framed sub-panels
- bento layouts that flatten importance
- center-aligned everything

### 6.2 The First Viewport Rule

The first viewport must read as one composition.

It should contain:
- one main statement
- one artifact or proof object
- one action
- one trust or evidence cue

It should not contain:
- three competing content zones
- too many stats
- several parallel CTAs

### 6.3 Surface Recipes

When designing a new module, choose one of these recipes and commit.

#### Recipe A: Instrument Card

Use for:
- artifact previews
- report modules
- high-attention product objects

Ingredients:
- white or near-white surface
- quiet border
- subtle vertical rhythm
- one accent entry point
- compact label treatment

#### Recipe B: Evidence Rail

Use for:
- methodology explanation
- supporting proof clusters
- research-to-product translations

Ingredients:
- open row
- minimal framing
- one divider
- small uppercase or mono-like label
- teal only where meaning changes

#### Recipe C: Warm Note

Use for:
- methodology aside
- trust clarifications
- research support callouts

Ingredients:
- restrained warm fill
- minimal border
- no large decorative icon
- compact copy

Use this sparingly.

### 6.4 Density

The current brand should move slightly denser.

Not cramped.
Not enterprise-heavy.

Just more instrument-like.

Key changes:
- tighter UI spacing
- shorter copy blocks
- less dead air around practical actions
- more decisive grouping

### 6.5 Horizontal Bias

RIYP benefits from left-aligned reading flow and two-part compositions.

This helps the brand feel:
- more authored
- less template-like
- more professional

Use center alignment only when it heightens a single statement.

### 6.6 Mobile Posture

On mobile, RIYP should not become "cute" or "simplified."

Preserve:
- the same narrative order
- one strong focal object
- the same contrast hierarchy
- clear action-first layout

Avoid:
- stacking too many framed blocks
- hiding the artifact
- turning proof into a carousel
- reducing all type contrast just to fit the viewport

## 7. Surfaces And Materials

### 7.1 Surface Model

Use three main surface families:

#### Instrument

For:
- homepage artifact
- report
- workspace modules

Feel:
- clean
- raised
- precise

#### Neutral Field

For:
- most page backgrounds
- structural transitions
- app chrome

Feel:
- calm
- unobtrusive
- slightly cool

#### Editorial Paper

For:
- research
- methodology
- selected trust contexts

Feel:
- warm
- quiet
- designed

### 7.2 Borders

Borders should do more work than shadows.

Preferred:
- quiet edge contrast
- subtle layered lines
- carefully tuned opacity

Avoid:
- dark obvious strokes everywhere
- thick card outlines
- fuzzy depth trying to do all the separation work

### 7.3 Shadows

Use shadows rarely and softly.

A RIYP shadow should feel like lift, not drama.

### 7.4 Radius

Current radius family is good.

Guidance:
- small radius for utility controls
- medium for product modules
- slightly larger only for hero or high-attention moments

Avoid over-rounding.
Too much roundness softens the brand.

### 7.5 Texture

Texture is optional.
If used, it should be nearly subliminal.

Allowed:
- faint grain on large editorial proof surfaces
- subtle paper variation inside research or methodology

Not allowed:
- obvious paper textures on product surfaces
- tactile backgrounds that compete with content
- retro print styling as brand identity

## 8. Motion Direction

### 8.1 Motion Role

Motion should:
- clarify sequence
- reveal judgment
- confirm state
- make expertise feel active

Motion should not:
- entertain
- bounce
- decorate flat layouts
- simulate delight for its own sake

### 8.2 Desired Motion Feel

- quiet confidence
- measured speed
- slight weight
- no theatrical wobble

### 8.3 Best Motion Patterns For RIYP

Use:
- scan reveals
- emphasis fades
- line replacement transitions
- progressive evidence build
- subtle value count or score fill when meaningful

Avoid:
- looping ambient effects
- elastic springs on structural UI
- floating decorative particles
- exaggerated parallax

### 8.4 Motion Hierarchy

#### Fast
- hover, press, focus
- 90ms to 140ms

#### Standard
- swaps, toggles, disclosure
- 180ms to 220ms

#### Signature
- artifact reveal
- evidence sequencing
- first-pass scan visual
- 300ms to 450ms

### 8.5 Motion Vocabulary

The motion vocabulary should be based on these verbs:

- reveal
- scan
- isolate
- replace
- confirm

It should not be based on:

- bounce
- float
- shimmer
- pulse for decoration
- playful spring-back

## 9. Illustration, Graphics, And Image System

### 9.1 Core Graphic Language

RIYP should use diagrams and markup, not generic SaaS illustration.

Best graphic ingredients:
- scan paths
- highlight bars
- underlines
- evidence callouts
- ranking ladders
- before/after transforms
- recruiter-lens overlays

### 9.2 Red Pen Language

The "red pen" should behave like a tool cue, not a whimsical mascot concept.

Good:
- decisive markup
- exact substitutions
- edit traces

Bad:
- schoolroom aesthetic
- scribbles for personality
- hand-drawn cuteness

### 9.2a Highlight Language

The preferred annotation system should feel analytical, not artistic.

Preferred marks:
- underline
- bracket
- thin highlight bar
- ranked badge
- inline replacement arrow

Avoid:
- marker scrawl
- jagged callout lines
- oversized "red pen" theatrics

### 9.3 Photography

Use photography sparingly.

If used:
- real founder or expert images
- candid and credible
- documentary-feeling

Avoid:
- smiling team grids
- stock recruiter scenes
- staged laptop photography

### 9.4 Product Imagery

The product itself should be the hero image system.

That means:
- artifact close-ups
- report states
- recruiter-lens overlays
- evidence rows
- line rewrites

The brand gets stronger every time the product looks like the proof.

## 10. Iconography And Marks

### 10.1 Icon Style

Icons should feel:
- geometric
- crisp
- low-ornament
- consistent in stroke

Prefer:
- 1.5px strokes
- controlled rounding
- simple forms

### 10.2 PocketMark

The current `PocketMark` is useful and serviceable.
It is not yet iconic enough to carry the brand by itself.

Recommendation:
- keep it for now
- use it more quietly
- do not force the entire aesthetic to orbit it

Potential future sprint:
- simplify the mark
- make the fold / pocket metaphor more distinctive
- test a more compact, higher-recall silhouette

### 10.3 Wordmark

The wordmark should remain elegant and relatively understated.

Do not bury it in over-styled nav chrome.
Let the surrounding system create the premium feeling.

## 11. Component-Level Finish

### 11.1 Buttons

Primary buttons should feel:
- compact
- definite
- crisp

They should not feel:
- pillowy
- oversized
- app-store cheerful

Preferred traits:
- strong contrast
- restrained radius
- minimal shadow
- obvious active state

### 11.2 Inputs

Inputs should feel:
- sober
- trustworthy
- easy to scan

Use:
- visible boundaries
- crisp labels
- clear focus state
- helpful placeholder copy only when useful

Avoid:
- faint ghost fields
- soft luxury styling
- low-contrast borders

### 11.3 Cards

The brand should not rely on cards for meaning.

If a card exists, it should have a reason:
- grouping
- emphasis
- interaction boundary

Do not wrap every idea in a container.

### 11.4 Tables, Evidence Rows, And Lists

These are brand opportunities.

Well-designed evidence rows can make RIYP feel more premium than any decorative hero treatment.

They should feel:
- exact
- aligned
- ordered
- readable in one glance

## 12. Surface-Specific Visual Guidance

### 11.1 Homepage

Feel:
- decisive
- slightly tense
- crisp
- product-first

Must include:
- one strong statement
- one artifact
- one immediate CTA
- one proof layer

Should not feel:
- warm and chatty
- blog-like
- over-explained

### 11.2 Workspace

Feel:
- calmer than marketing
- more exact
- faster
- denser

The workspace should feel like a consultation instrument, not a dashboard.

### 11.3 Report

Feel:
- fair
- precise
- legible
- non-shaming

The report is the main product object.
It should receive the highest craft bar in the system.

### 11.4 Research

Feel:
- editorial
- high-craft
- proof-rich
- explanatory

This is the right place for warm paper and designed longform rhythm.

### 11.5 Trust, Security, And Legal

Feel:
- clear
- controlled
- plainspoken
- surprisingly calm

These pages should not try to prove personality.
They should prove maturity.

### 12.6 Pricing

Feel:
- precise
- transparent
- low-drama

The design should communicate:
- no trap
- no gotcha
- clear boundary between free and paid

### 12.7 Extension Surfaces

The extension should feel like a tactical companion to the main product, not a smaller separate brand.

Visual goals:
- tighter density
- direct state communication
- no decorative warmth
- obvious job context capture

The extension is where the brand can lean slightly more operational, but it should still feel like RIYP.

## 13. Things To Delete From The Current Feel

- desk and drawer metaphors as repeated emotional cues
- boutique warmth as the main site atmosphere
- too much reliance on paper texture to signal taste
- decorative cleverness in support surfaces
- any component that feels more like brand theater than product clarity

## 14. Things To Push Harder

- product artifact realism
- typographic confidence
- evidence styling
- exact line-level transformation moments
- denser and more premium app chrome
- cooler, cleaner page composition

## 15. Final Visual Gut Check

Ask these questions before shipping:

1. Does this look like a premium tool with judgment?
2. Is the serif doing decisive work instead of decorative work?
3. Is teal acting like emphasis instead of atmosphere?
4. Is the page led by a real product object or by layout chrome?
5. Does this feel more exact than editorial?
6. Would this still feel specific if all the copy were hidden?
